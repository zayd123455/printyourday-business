# PrintYourDay for wedding planners

## Client Brief

**Client:** Adam, founder of PrintYourDay, a studio in Mount Vernon, NY that prints
photos at 40 by 60 inches on rigid foam board for events.

**Purpose:** A site that tells wedding planners what the boards are and gets them to
call Adam.

**Audience:** Wedding planners and venue coordinators in Westchester and the New York
area. They open it on a phone between vendors and need to know in a few seconds
whether this studio can do big photo boards for an event, what it costs, and how fast.

**Key action:** Call Adam.

**Pages needed:**
- Home, aimed at planners
- Gallery of boards at real events
- How it works and pricing
- Contact

**Content status:**
- Have: the board spec, the upload and proof process, the prices (The Three $500,
  The Five $750, Jumbo by quote, 48 hour rush $50 a board), delivery zones, the
  phone number, and six photos of boards at events.
- Still need from Adam: planner or trade pricing and minimum order, studio hours
  and the address to show publicly, one quote from a planner or couple, and more
  wedding photos (only one of the six is a wedding).

**Style preferences:** Adam left the look to me. He wants it to match the PrintYourDay
brand he already uses: cream background, dark text, sage green accent, Outfit font.
Photo led and simple, no animation. The one hard rule from him: the name of his other
company must not appear anywhere on the site.

**Inspiration sites:** Adam did not name any, so these are mine, picked for a planner
audience:
- minted.com wedding signs. Big photos, white space, one price under each item. The
  layout I want for the pricing section.
- theknot.com vendor pages. How a planner actually browses a supplier: gallery first,
  one contact button, nothing else competing with it.
- signs.com foam board pages. A plain print vendor. Useful as the opposite: specs and
  a quote form, no photos of the product in use. Ours should show the boards at events.

## Layout Plan

Sketch: `sketch.jpg`, committed in this repo.

Description given to the AI agent as the starting prompt:

> Four pages with the same header: the PrintYourDay name on the left, four nav links,
> and the phone number as a button on the right. Home opens with one full-width
> wedding photo and one sentence, then three short reasons for a planner (proof
> before print, 48 hour rush, delivery to the venue), then the two prices, then the
> phone number again. Gallery is a grid of six captioned photos, two across on a
> phone. How it works is three numbered steps and a price list. Contact is the phone,
> the email, the delivery zones and the studio town. Footer on every page repeats the
> phone and email.
