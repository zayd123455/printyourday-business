# PrintYourDay for wedding planners

## Client Brief

**Client:** Adam, who runs PrintYourDay in Mount Vernon, NY. He prints photos at
40 by 60 inches on rigid foam board for events. My agency already does work for
him, so I know the business and he was easy to reach.

**Purpose:** A site that shows wedding planners what the boards are and gets them
to call Adam.

**Audience:** Wedding planners and venue coordinators around Westchester and New
York. They open it on a phone between two other vendors. In a few seconds they
need to know that he does big photo boards, what they cost and how fast he can
turn them around.

**Key action:** Call Adam.

**Pages needed:**
- Home, written for planners
- Gallery, boards at real events
- How it works and pricing
- Contact

**Content status:** Have: the board spec, how ordering works (upload a photo,
approve a proof, free reprint if it does not match), the prices, the delivery
zones, the phone number, and six photos of boards at events. Still need from
Adam: trade pricing and a minimum order, studio hours and the address he wants
public, a quote from a planner who used the boards, and more wedding photos.
Only one of the six is a wedding.

**Style preferences:** Adam left the look to me. Match what he already uses for
PrintYourDay: cream background, dark text, sage green, the Outfit font. Photos
first, lots of space, nothing moving. One hard rule from him: the name of his
other company does not appear anywhere on this site.

**Inspiration sites:** Adam said he trusts me on this, so these are my picks for
a planner audience:
- minted.com, wedding signs. Big photos, white space, one price under each item.
  That is the layout I want for pricing.
- theknot.com, vendor pages. How a planner actually browses a supplier: gallery
  first, one contact button, nothing else competing with it.
- signs.com, foam board. The opposite. Specs and a quote form, no photos of the
  product in use. Ours has to show the boards at events.

## Layout Plan

Sketch: `sketch.jpg` in this repo. Home page on a phone and on a laptop.

The description I gave the agent as the starting prompt:

> Four pages with the same header: PrintYourDay on the left, four nav links, and
> the phone number as a button on the right. Home opens with one full-width
> wedding photo and one sentence, then three short reasons for a planner (proof
> before print, 48 hour rush, delivery to the venue), then the two prices, then
> the phone number again. Gallery is a grid of six captioned photos, two across
> on a phone. How it works is three numbered steps and a price list. Contact is
> the phone, the email, the delivery zones and the studio town. Footer on every
> page repeats the phone and email.
